What is happening

The questionWhich way must a gravity-gradient satellite fly to stay pointed at the Earth, and what does it do when it is disturbed?

—

    New to this? Key terms
    Gravity gradient
    Gravity weakens with distance, so the end of a satellite nearest the Earth is pulled slightly harder than the far end. On a tilted satellite that difference twists it toward the vertical.
    Local vertical, nadir
    The line from the satellite to the center of the Earth. Nadir is straight down along it; zenith is straight up.
    Orbit frame
    Axes that travel with the satellite: ô1 along the flight direction, ô3 toward the Earth, ô2 completing the set (opposite the orbit normal). It turns once per orbit.
    Roll, pitch, yaw
    Rotations about the flight direction, the orbit normal and the local vertical. The moments of inertia about those axes are Ir, Ip and Iy.
    Libration
    A slow back-and-forth swing about the vertical, like a pendulum, with periods of tens of minutes.
    Stiffness and gyroscopic stability
    Stable with stiffness means every tilt is pushed back, like a spring. Gyroscopic stability means the motion only circles around, held by the turning of the orbit; any energy loss undoes it.
    Damper
    Anything that turns the swinging into heat: a fluid or eddy-current damper on the boom, magnetic hysteresis rods, or magnetic torquers.
