Wheels

Three wheels on the principal axes, so h̄w = hs and Aw = 1. A wheel at hmax cannot take more momentum in that direction: it is saturated. Dumping fires thrusters while the wheels slow.

Disturbance

Each run picks where it acts. Pitch, in body (a misaligned thruster): fixed along an axis that keeps its direction in space, so it fills the pitch wheel steadily. Roll, in body: the roll axis swings round once per orbit, so the momentum it adds keeps changing direction and never builds past 2gd/ωc. Fixed in space (like solar pressure), in the orbit plane: it adds momentum in one direction in space, which the roll and yaw wheels must take turns holding, and it builds up.

    New to this? Key terms
    Reaction wheel
    A flywheel driven by an electric motor. Spinning it up one way turns the satellite the other way.
    Momentum exchange
    Wheels move angular momentum between themselves and the body; they never add or remove any. Only outside torques, such as gravity gradient or thrusters, change the total.
    LVLH (orbit frame)
    Local vertical, local horizontal: axes that keep one face of the satellite toward the Earth. They turn once per orbit.
    Gyroscopic coupling
    Stored momentum carried around by a turning body produces a torque across the turn. It ties the axes together even when no wheel is being torqued.
    Bias momentum
    Wheel momentum kept on purpose, usually on the pitch axis, so wheels avoid passing through zero speed.
    Saturation
    A wheel at its top speed. It cannot absorb more momentum in that direction, so the satellite can no longer be held.
    Momentum dumping
    Using an outside torque, thrusters or magnetic torquers, while the wheels slow down, so the stored momentum leaves the spacecraft.
