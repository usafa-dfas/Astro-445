What is happening

The questionHow big a disturbance can a gravity-gradient satellite absorb before it tumbles, and how does a damper settle it?

—

    New to this? Key terms
    Libration
    A swing back and forth about the local vertical, like a pendulum. A gravity-gradient satellite librates with a period of about an hour.
    Tumbling
    Turning end over end relative to the Earth, because the satellite had too much energy to be turned back.
    Phase plane
    A plot of rate against angle. Each motion traces a curve; closed loops are librations, wavy lines running across are tumbles.
    Separatrix
    The curve between the two kinds of motion. It passes through the unstable, horizontal attitudes at ±90°.
    Damping ratio ζ
    How strongly the damper resists the swinging, compared with what would stop it in one swing (ζ = 1).
    Capture
    A tumbling satellite losing enough energy to start librating instead. It may end with the boom toward the Earth or pointing away.

    Checks worth running

      How damping is provided

      • Passive mechanical dampers. A pendulum, spring-mass or hinged second boom at the tip, coupled through viscous fluid or eddy currents. Internal, so like Lesson 25's damper rotors it works best tuned to the libration frequency.
      • Magnetic hysteresis rods. Soft magnetic rods magnetized back and forth by the Earth's field as the satellite swings, losing a little energy each cycle. No moving parts, no power; common on small satellites.
      • Active magnetic damping. Magnetic torquers driven by rate or magnetometer readings. An outside torque; the rate model here is its average effect, though torquers can push only across the local field.

      Roll and yaw need damping too, and only the arrangement Ip > Ir > Iy survives it (Lesson 29). The Gravity-Gradient Explorer's "Gyroscopic only" preset shows the other case. In an eccentric orbit the orbit frame turns unevenly and drives a forced libration once per orbit, which damping limits but cannot remove.

      Model

      Large-angle pitch, with the damper
      Ipθ̈ + cdθ̇ + 3ωc2(Ir − Iy) sin θ cos θ = 0
      k2 = (Ir − Iy)/Ip,  ωl = √(3k2) ωc
      Energy and the separatrix
      E = ½θ̇2 + (3/2)ωc2k2 sin2θ,  Esep = (3/2)ωc2k2
      tumbles if |θ̇| > ωl at θ = 0;  dE/dt = −(cd/Ip)θ̇2
      Damper and periods
      ζ = cd/(2Ipωl),  time constant 1/(ζωl),  ωd = ωl√(1 − ζ2)
      undamped swing of amplitude θm: period 4K(sin θm)/ωl

      The simulation integrates the pitch equation exactly as written, with fixed-step RK4, in a circular orbit (μ = 398 600 km³/s², RE = 6378 km). The capture map integrates the same equation in units of 1/ωl, where only ζ matters. "Captured" is the moment the energy first falls below Esep; after that the satellite can only settle in the well it is in.
