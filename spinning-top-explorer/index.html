Spinning Top Explorer

A symmetric top on a fixed, frictionless pivot, described by 3-1-3 Euler angles in the inertial frame FA. Gravity's torque about the pivot is always horizontal, so the total energy E and the vertical momentum hẐ stay constant. The axis precesses about Ẑ while it nods between θmin and θmax.

What is happening

—

    New to this? Key terms
    Pivot
    The fixed point the top leans on. Gravity pulls its center of mass, a distance l from the pivot, straight down.
    Spin
    The disk turning about its own axle. The faster the spin, the stiffer the top.
    Gravity torque ḡc
    The twist gravity applies about the pivot, trying to tip the top over. It points sideways, not down.
    Angular momentum h̄
    The top's "amount of spin", mostly along the axle when it spins fast. A torque changes h̄ in the direction of the torque, so a sideways torque swings h̄ sideways instead of tipping it down.
    Precession
    The slow swing of the axle around the vertical. This is how a spinning top answers gravity.
    Nutation
    A nodding of the axle up and down between two angles, θmin and θmax, on top of the precession.
    Apex trace
    The path the tip of the axle draws. Its shape (waves, loops or cusps) shows how precession and nodding combine.
    Gyroscopic stiffness
    A spinning body's resistance to being tipped. Spacecraft spin for the same reason, to hold their pointing against disturbances.

    Model

    Euler's equations about the pivot
    Itω1 = (It − Ia) ω2ω3 + g1
    Itω2 = (Ia − It) ω1ω3 + g2
    Iaω3 = 0,   ḡc = −mgl (p̂3 × Ẑ)
    Constants of motion
    E = ½It(θ² + ψ² sin²θ) + ½Iaω30² + mgl cos θ
    hẐ = Itψ sin²θ + Iaω30 cos θ
    Released from rest
    cos θmax = a/2 − √(1 − a cos θ0 + (a/2)²)
    ψ = (cos θ0 − cos θ) Iaω30It sin²θ

    The motion is integrated with fixed-step RK4 on Euler's equations and a quaternion, so it stays valid at θ = 0 where the Euler angles are singular. The turning points come from the cubic in cos θ built from E and hẐ, which works for any initial rates. The exact nutation period and mean ψ are integrals over one nod between those roots.
